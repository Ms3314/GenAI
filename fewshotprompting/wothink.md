Great goal. You can boost your odds a lot in 6 months with a focused, action-driven plan. I can’t promise “guaranteed” internships—markets vary and competition is real—but you can maximise your chances by building a solid portfolio, getting visible, and practicing for interviews.

Here’s a clear, actionable 6-month plan you can start today.

Core pillars
- Skills & portfolio: 3-4 solid projects that showcase your strengths (coding, problem solving, and a touch of real-world impact).
- Resume/portfolio presence: clean resume, LinkedIn, GitHub, and a simple personal site.
- Job search & applications: targeted outreach, many well-crafted applications, and follow-ups.
- Interview readiness: coding practice, basic system-design awareness (if applicable), and mock interviews.
- Networking & referrals: alumni, professors, career fairs, and open-source contributors.

6-month month-by-month plan

Month 1 (now)
- Clarify focus: decide 1–2 areas (e.g., Backend/Frontend/Algorithms/Data Structures/ML basics).
- Resume & profiles: one-page resume updated; LinkedIn polished; GitHub clean; set up a simple personal site/portfolio.
- Projects: start 2 small, impactful projects that you can finish in 6–8 weeks. Examples:
  - Project A: a full-stack web app or a data-processing script with clear user impact.
  - Project B: a command-line tool or library (preferably open-source-friendly) or a small ML/DS project.
- Coding practice: start a structured plan (e.g., 4–6 days/week, 60–90 minutes sessions). Do easy-to-medium DS/Algo problems to build confidence.
- Open-source: identify 1 starter issue in a public repo and contribute something meaningful this month.

Month 2
- Finish initial projects or reach a solid MVP.
- Documentation: write clear READMEs, tech stack notes, and a short “impact” section for each project (what it does, tech used, what you learned).
- Portfolio push: deploy at least one project (GitHub Pages, Netlify, Heroku, or similar) and link from your resume.
- Applications: start applying broadly (target 15–30 well-matched roles per week). Include internships at startups, product companies, and larger tech firms that run summer internships.
- Networking: reach out to 5–7 alumni or seniors weekly for quick chats about internships and referrals.

Month 3
- Interview prep ramp-up: daily practice on coding problems; start basic system-design prep if you’re aiming for software roles with systems work.
- Mock interviews: schedule at least 2–3 mock interviews (peer or platform-based) per week.
- Refine resumes: tailor a resume for 2–3 target roles; keep a short version ready for quick applications.
- Campus/partners: participate in campus career fairs or online company info sessions; collect interviewer names for follow-ups.

Month 4
- Active interview phase: aim to land at least 2–3 interview chances per week (via referrals, campus drives, or direct applications).
- Projects: if time allows, add a small feature to an project or start a new 3-week “pilot” project aligned with your target roles.
- Certifications or short courses (optional): if relevant to your field (e.g., cloud basics, web security, or data basics), complete a short, reputable credential to stand out.

Month 5
- Offers and negotiations prep: if you’ve got interviews, practice behavioral questions and high-level design questions; prepare to discuss your projects in detail.
- Narrow choices: if you receive multiple offers, compare roles, teams, learning opportunities, mentorship, location, and compensation; seek clarity on internship duties, start date, and expected deliverables.
- If not yet landed: broaden search to remote-friendly internships, international programs, and smaller startups; consider paid micro-internships or research assistant positions with a professor if applicable.

Month 6
- Decision and prep: decide on the best-fit internship; set up logistics (onboarding, tools, team intro).
- Deliverables plan: outline what you’ll deliver in your first 6–8 weeks to prove you’re contributing meaningfully.
- If no offer: continue applying, but also explore related avenues (research assistantships, helping with a professor’s project, freelancing, or tech support roles) to keep momentum.

Practical tips that move the needle
- Build a strong portfolio: 3-4 well-documented projects is better than many half-done ones. Focus on quality, not quantity.
- Make your applications easy to read: one-page resume, bullet-point impact statements, quantify results where possible (e.g., reduced load time by 30%, handled data for X users).
- Tailor every resume/cover letter to the role. Highlight relevant projects and problems you solved.
- Networking matters: try to talk to at least 5–7 people per week (alumni, internship coordinators, professors, peers who’ve interned). Ask for referrals after a brief, concrete chat.
- Show real-world impact: emphasize what you built, how it’s used, and what you learned (not just features).
- Soft skills count: practice clear explanations of your projects and your decision-making in interviews.
- Stay organized: track applications, interviews, follow-ups, and outcomes in a simple spreadsheet.
